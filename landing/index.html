Security

Your data stays in your car.
It never reaches our servers.

GlucoPilot has no server, account or analytics of its own. Data flows only between the source you choose and your phone.

  • HTTPS required. Cleartext traffic is fully disabled; http URLs are rejected.
  • No redirects. 3xx responses are rejected; the API key cannot leak to another host.
  • No raw key storage. Only the SHA-1 digest required by the Nightscout protocol is kept.
  • Validated data. Readings that fail range and time checks never reach the UI.
  • No tracking. No advertising ID, analytics SDK or cloud backup.
  • Write-only health export. Health Connect access is optional, granted by you and never read back; revoking it stops the export.
  • Portable settings. The export file carries the Nightscout digest, never the raw key.

Setup

Three steps to the cockpit.

GlucoPilot reads the CGM app you already use. One system permission, no configuration inside that app, and xDrip+ or Nightscout only if you want them.

  1. 01

    Allow the source

    Tap “Allow notification access” once so GlucoPilot can read the sensor app's values. No setting inside Dexcom, Libre, xDrip+ or any other CGM app.

  2. 02

    Set your range

    Choose your low and high thresholds. The default is 70–180 mg/dL; urgent bands are fixed at 54 and 250.

  3. 03

    Open it in the car

    Plug in and Android Auto starts GlucoPilot's monitoring by itself. Open the cockpit for value, trend and source age.

Open source. Your data, your choices.

The Android side follows Google's Car App Library sample architecture; the CGM layer reads the sensor app's notifications, with the xDrip+ broadcast contract and the Nightscout REST API as optional sources.

Frequently asked questions

FAQ

Common questions.

Is GlucoPilot a medical device?

No. It is a viewing and reminder tool; it does not recommend treatment or insulin doses. Always consult your healthcare professional and your sensor's official app for diagnosis and treatment decisions.

Does it connect directly to Dexcom or Libre?

It does not talk to the sensor itself. It reads the value your sensor app already shows, through Android's notification access (granted once). That keeps it running alongside the maker's app without conflicts; xDrip+ and Nightscout work too.

Is Nightscout required?

No. GlucoPilot reads your sensor app directly on the phone. Nightscout is optional for remote monitoring or multi-device setups.

Where does my data go?

Only to the source you choose. GlucoPilot has no server of its own; there is no analytics, advertising or third-party tracking SDK. Settings and the last drive's four numbers stay on the device. If you turn on Health Connect, new readings are written there as blood glucose records — nothing is read back — and the settings export only leaves the phone when you choose a file.

Do alerts distract me while driving?

Alerts are sent only when they matter: a range alert at most once every ten minutes for the same band, a “Low soon” / “High soon” projection at most once every half hour, and an urgent low every five minutes up to three times until you tap “Got it”. Mute silences all of them for 30 minutes. The car screen also follows driver-safety rules and shows a limited number of lines.

Can I see my glucose while navigation or music is open?

Yes, but not as a persistent window: Android Auto does not allow third-party apps to keep a window on top of Maps or media. GlucoPilot uses a silent, persistent car notification instead; the value updates with every reading, a short alert appears out of range, and tapping the notification opens the cockpit.

Does it warn me before I go low or high?

Yes, when the trend is clear enough. GlucoPilot fits the last 15 minutes of readings (at least three) and only speaks when the slope is at least 0.5 mg/dL per minute and would reach your low within 30 minutes or your high within 45. It is a straight-line projection of what already happened, not a prediction model and not dosing advice, and it is limited to once per half hour.

Does GlucoPilot write to Health Connect?

Only if you turn it on and grant the permission. It writes new readings as interstitial fluid blood glucose records; it never reads health data back. You can revoke access in Health Connect at any time, and after that nothing new is written.

What does it keep from a drive?

Four small numbers: minutes, time in range, the lowest value and how many readings were low. They stay in the app's own settings on the phone, with no server and no upload, and are shown on the setup screen as “Last drive”.

Which Android versions are supported?

Android 8.0 (API 26) and above. For Android Auto, your phone needs the Android Auto app and a compatible car or the Desktop Head Unit. Health Connect export needs Android 9 or the Health Connect app.

What's new

New in 1.2.0

The cockpit becomes a co-pilot: it warns before you leave range, draws the last 90 minutes, explains what the sensor itself is doing, checks the setup before you drive, and keeps a small summary of every drive on the phone. Everything below is in the signed APK on this page.

1.2.0

Warns before you leave range

Reads the last 15 minutes as a straight-line trend. If your low is about 30 minutes away — 45 for a high — one “Low soon” or “High soon” alert appears, at most every half hour and only while driving. It is a projection of the measured slope, not dosing advice.

1.2.0

The last 90 minutes, drawn

The cockpit now shows your trend line over a soft target band, so the shape of the climb or fall is visible at a glance. Time in range is counted from the moment this drive started, so the percentage matches the drive you are in.

1.2.0

Knows what the sensor is doing

Warm-up, signal loss and a finished sensor are read from the CGM app's own notifications, so a gap is explained instead of looking like a dead connection. Sensor battery is shown in the cockpit, with one quiet notice at 15% or less.

1.2.0

An urgent low that does not give up

At 54 mg/dL or below the alert repeats every five minutes, at most three times, and each repeat alerts again instead of updating silently. “Got it” on the notification quiets it for half an hour.

1.2.0

Sensor change, one tap

The “No glucose data” reminder now carries a “Sensor change” action that pauses it for two hours — from the car notification or the phone button, with a live countdown. The reminder returns on its own if the sensor is still silent afterwards.

1.2.0

A setup check before you drive

Four rows on the phone show whether notification access, notification permission, a live CGM value and the car connection are working — so a silent notification is caught in the driveway, not on the motorway.

1.2.0

Your drive, written down

When monitoring stops, the phone keeps the drive's minutes, time in range, lowest value and low count. Four small numbers, stored locally with no upload, so you can compare drives without exporting anything.

1.2.0

Health Connect and a settings backup

Optional, write-only Health Connect export of new readings: you grant it, you can revoke it, and GlucoPilot never reads health data back. Settings can be exported to a file and imported on a new phone; the file carries the Nightscout digest, never the raw key.

1.1.0

Reads any CGM app

Dexcom, Libre, Diabox, xDrip+ — whatever posts the value in a notification, GlucoPilot picks it up. One system permission, and no settings to change inside the CGM app.

1.1.0

Only runs in the car

Monitoring and the status notification start the moment Android Auto connects, and stop when the phone is unplugged. Nothing is started at boot, so the app stays quiet away from the car.

1.1.1

Shows why a value is missing

When nothing reaches the car, Source check lists the last reading with its app and the last ten notifications, each marked accepted or rejected with the reason.

1.1.2

Warns when the sensor goes quiet

If no new reading arrives for 15 minutes, a single high-priority “No glucose data” alert appears in the car and clears itself when readings return — so an old value never looks current.

1.1.3

Quiet through a sensor change

The “No glucose data” alert now waits for a reading that arrives during the drive, so a warming-up sensor no longer sets it off. A new “I’m changing my sensor” button pauses the reminder for two hours and counts the minutes down while the screen is open.
